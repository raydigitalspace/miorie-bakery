The sourdough story

Good bread
takes time.

Made for those who appreciate bread with a little more character.

Three things go into every mio·rie loaf: a considered blend of grains, a great deal of time, and a little craft.

Rustic tradition. Naturally leavened. And very much a labour of love.

The flour

Premium European flours, stone-ground spelt and rye.

Read more

Sourdough at mio·rie begins with a carefully curated blend of premium European flours and stone-ground whole grains, including spelt and rye. Each grain brings its own unique characteristics, contributing to the depth of flavour, texture and complexity in our bread.

The time

An average of 40 hours. Never rushed.

Read more

We then give our dough time.

Slow-fermented under controlled temperatures for an average of 40 hours, the process is intentional and never rushed. The long, cold maturation allows the deepening of flavour, giving each loaf its distinctive sourdough character.

The boulangerie

Two ways we
bake sourdough.

Every loaf shares the same foundation: European flours, stone-ground spelt and rye, and around 40 hours of cold fermentation. What changes is what we fold into it.

One for the daily table. One for a little indulgence.

Everyday sourdough

From S$15

The daily table. Celebrating the classic tradition, time-crafted, and made for everyday enjoyment.

Read more

In the dough. Flour, water, salt and starter, with classic additions: toasted seeds, cranberry and walnut, or cheese and jalapeño.

On the palate. Earthy, mildly tangy, open and chewy. Familiar flavours with a distinctive sourdough tang.

Made for. Morning toast, sandwiches, kaya, the week’s table.

Artisan sourdough

S$22

Rustic, yet luxurious. Crafted with character, and a little indulgence.

Read more

In the dough. Eggs and French butter, laminated like a croissant. Sun-dried Turkish figs steeped in dark rum. Pecans activated, slow-dried and lightly toasted in house.

On the palate. Rich but not heavy. Brioche-soft or boldly textured, always unmistakably sourdough.

Made for. Gifting, slow weekends, and bread you eat on its own.
